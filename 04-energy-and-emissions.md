# Energy Content & Environmental Impact of Biofuels

## Energy Content: BTU and Energy Density

### The Numbers That Matter for Fuel Economy

| Fuel | BTU/gallon | MJ/liter | % of Gasoline | % of Diesel |
|------|-----------|----------|---------------|-------------|
| Gasoline (regular) | 114,100 | 31.8 | 100% | 88% |
| Diesel #2 | 129,500 | 36.1 | 113% | 100% |
| **Ethanol (E100)** | 76,330 | 21.3 | 67% | 59% |
| E10 (10% ethanol) | 111,800 | 31.2 | 98% | 86% |
| E85 (85% ethanol) | 81,800 | 22.8 | 72% | 63% |
| **Biodiesel (B100)** | 118,300 | 33.0 | 104% | 91% |
| B20 (20% biodiesel) | 126,700 | 35.3 | 111% | 98% |
| Butanol | 105,000 | 29.3 | 92% | 81% |
| Propane (LPG) | 84,250 | 23.5 | 74% | 65% |
| Natural gas (CNG) | 900 BTU/ft³ | — | — | — |

### What This Means for Driving

- **E85 flex fuel** contains 15-27% less energy per gallon than gasoline —
  you'll get fewer miles per tank
- **B100 biodiesel** has about 9% less energy per gallon than petroleum
  diesel but burns cleaner
- **Butanol** is a better gasoline replacement than ethanol (higher
  energy content, less corrosive, can blend at higher ratios)
- **Renewable diesel** has the same energy content as petroleum diesel —
  no fuel economy penalty at all

## Fossil Energy Ratio (FER)

FER = energy output ÷ fossil energy input. A value >1 means you get more
energy out than fossil energy you put in.

| Fuel | Fossil Energy Ratio (FER) |
|------|---------------------------|
| Gasoline | 0.8 (energy sink — takes more fossil energy to produce than you get) |
| Diesel (petroleum) | 0.83 (energy sink) |
| Corn ethanol (US average) | ~1.5 |
| Corn ethanol (best: Iowa/Minnesota) | ~4.0 |
| Sugarcane ethanol (Brazil) | ~2.1 to 8.0 (estimates vary) |
| Cellulosic ethanol (corn stover) | 4.4 |
| Cellulosic ethanol (poplar trees) | 4.5 |
| Cellulosic ethanol (various feedstocks) | 6.6 |
| Biodiesel (soybean) | 4.6 |
| Biodiesel (palm oil) | 3.1 |
| Biodiesel (microalgae) | 3.0 |
| Biodiesel (jatropha) | 15.0 |

> Historical note: Corn ethanol was actually an energy SINK (FER < 1)
> in the 1980s-1990s. Improvements in farming efficiency and ethanol
> plant technology flipped it to a net gain by the mid-2000s.

|---------|------------------------------|-------|

| Sugarcane ethanol | -60% to -70% | Much better than corn ethanol |
| Cellulosic ethanol | -93% to -97% | Including/excluding land use change |

| B20 biodiesel blend | -15% | vs. petroleum diesel |

## Other Environmental Impacts

### Water Consumption

Biofuel production is FAR more water-intensive than fossil fuels.

| Fuel | Water Consumption (gal per GJ of fuel) |
|------|---------------------------------------|
| Conventional oil | 0.63 - 632 |
| Conventional natural gas | 0.63 - 3.2 |
| Corn ethanol (rain-fed) | 13 - 110 |
| Corn ethanol (irrigated) | 500 - 2,700 |
| Soybean biodiesel (irrigated) | 6.3 - 6,320 |
| EU ethanol/biodiesel average | 40-60x more than fossil fuels |

> Over 80% of water used in corn-ethanol production is for irrigation.
> In Nebraska, it's 99%.

### Fertilizer Runoff & Dead Zones

- Increasing corn acreage for ethanol → more nitrogen fertilizer
- Nitrogen runoff into the Mississippi River → **Gulf of Mexico hypoxic dead zone**
- 2024 forecast: **5,827 square miles** (size of Connecticut)
- Perennial energy crops (switchgrass, miscanthus) don't have this problem

### Land Use Change & Deforestation

- **Direct:** Converting forests/grasslands to biofuel crop fields
- **Indirect (iLUC):** Higher crop prices motivate farmers elsewhere to
  clear land, releasing stored soil/forest carbon
- Palm oil biodiesel: clearing tropical rainforests in Indonesia/Malaysia
  releases massive amounts of carbon — may be worse than fossil diesel
  for decades

### Land Projections

- Currently ~2.5% of global arable land used for biofuels
- Projected to rise to **~6% by 2050**
- This matters enormously for food prices, biodiversity, and water

### Biodiversity

| Cropping System | Biodiversity Impact |
|----------------|---------------------|
| Corn monoculture | Very low (single species, high inputs) |
| Soybean monoculture | Very low |
| Palm oil plantation | Very low (replaces rainforest) |
| Sugarcane plantation | Low |
| Perennial grasses (switchgrass) | Moderate-High (bird habitat, pollinators) |
| Mixed native prairie | High (restores ecosystem) |

### Air Quality

- **Ethanol blends:** Reduce CO and some toxics, but may increase
  ground-level ozone formation
- **Biodiesel:** Reduces particulate matter, CO, and unburned
  hydrocarbons; slight NOx increase
- **B100 PM reduction:** ~47% less particulate matter
- **B100 CO reduction:** ~48% less carbon monoxide

## The "Food vs. Fuel" Debate in Numbers

- **36% of US corn crop** went to ethanol in 2024/25 (~5.5 billion bushels)
- Biofuel demand contributed **20-50% to corn price increases** during
  the 2006-2008 food price spikes
- Global biofuel growth expected to raise corn/wheat prices 1-2%,
  vegetable oil prices ~5-8% through 2030
- One tank of ethanol in an SUV (~25 gallons) uses enough corn to feed
  one person for a year

## Energy Return Timeline

How long it takes to "pay back" the carbon debt from land conversion:

| Land Conversion | Carbon Payback Time |
|----------------|--------------------|
| Corn ethanol on existing US cropland | Near-immediate |
| Corn ethanol on converted grassland | ~50-90 years |
| Soy biodiesel on converted Amazon rainforest | ~300+ years |
| Palm oil on converted peatland rainforest | ~400-800+ years |
| Cellulosic ethanol on marginal land | Near-immediate |
| Algae on desert land | Near-immediate (no land conversion) |

This is a crucial game mechanic! Converting the wrong type of land can create
a carbon debt that takes centuries to repay.
