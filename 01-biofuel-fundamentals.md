# Biofuel Fundamentals: Types, Generations, and Overview

## What Are Biofuels?

Biofuels are fuels derived from **biomass** — plant material, algae, or animal waste. Because
biomass can be regrown and replenished, biofuels are considered a **renewable energy** source,
unlike fossil fuels (petroleum, coal, natural gas).

- **Primary use:** Transportation (95% of all biofuel use)
- **Global share:** ~1% of world energy, ~4% of global transport energy
- **US share:** ~2% of US energy, ~10% of vehicle fuel by volume
- **Demand growth:** Up 27% from 2018 to 2023

## The Four Generations of Biofuels

### 1st Generation — Food Crops

Made from food crops like corn, sugarcane, soybeans, and palm oil.

| Type | Feedstock | Process |
|------|-----------|---------|
| Ethanol | Corn (US), Sugarcane (Brazil) | Fermentation of sugars/starches |
| Biodiesel | Soybean oil, palm oil, rapeseed | Transesterification |

**Pros:** Simple technology, widely deployed, reduces petroleum dependence.
**Cons:** Competes with food supply ("food vs. fuel"), can cause deforestation,
limited GHG reduction (corn ethanol: ~39% less than gasoline).

### 2nd Generation — Non-Food Biomass (Advanced Biofuels)

Uses waste materials and non-food crops.

| Type | Feedstock | Process |
|------|-----------|---------|
| Cellulosic ethanol | Corn stover, wood chips, switchgrass | Enzymatic hydrolysis + fermentation |
| Renewable diesel | Waste cooking oil, animal fats | Hydrotreating |

**Pros:** No food competition, uses waste, much lower carbon footprint
(cellulosic ethanol: 97% less GHG than gasoline).
**Cons:** More expensive to produce, complex technology, not yet at large scale.

### 3rd Generation — Algae

Algae and cyanobacteria grown in ponds or photobioreactors.

- Some algae species have **40% lipid content by weight**
- Can yield **10–100x more fuel per acre** than land crops
- Up to **61,000 liters of biodiesel per hectare**
- Can grow in wastewater, saltwater, on non-arable land

**Pros:** Extremely high yield, no land competition, can absorb CO₂.
**Cons:** Expensive harvesting, energy-intensive processing, not commercially viable yet.

### 4th Generation — Genetically Engineered Organisms

Uses CRISPR and genetic engineering to modify bacteria, yeast, or algae
to directly produce advanced fuels (butanol, bio-hydrocarbons) from CO₂ and sunlight.

**Pros:** Potentially carbon-negative, highest theoretical yields.
**Cons:** Still in R&D, not deployed at scale.

## Main Types of Liquid Biofuels

### Ethanol (82% of US biofuel production)

- Alcohol fuel made by fermenting sugars from corn, sugarcane, or cellulose
- Blended with gasoline: **E10** (10% ethanol, works in all cars), **E85** (85% ethanol, flex-fuel only)
- US produced **15.36 billion gallons** in 2022
- US + Brazil = 80% of global ethanol production
- 94% of US ethanol comes from corn (36% of the US corn supply in 2024/25)
- US has **187 ethanol refineries** with 18,010 million gal/yr total capacity

### Biodiesel (9% of US biofuel production)

- Made from vegetable oils, animal fats, or waste cooking oil via **transesterification**
- Blended with diesel: **B5**, **B20** (20% biodiesel), or **B100** (pure)
- US produced **1.62 billion gallons** in 2022
- 56 biodiesel plants in the US (2024)
- Feedstocks: 30% palm oil, 22% soy, 14% used cooking oil, 12% rapeseed, 9% animal fats (globally)

### Renewable Diesel (8% of US biofuel production)

- Chemically **identical** to petroleum diesel — a true "drop-in" fuel
- Made by hydrotreating vegetable oils and fats (different process than biodiesel)
- California consumes **97% of all US renewable diesel**
- Feedstocks: 52% waste cooking oil/animal fats, 36% vegetable oils

### Sustainable Aviation Fuel (SAF) — 0.1% of global production (emerging)

- Drop-in replacement for jet fuel made from biomass
- IRA provides $1.75/gal tax credit for SAF production

### Biogas / Biomethane

- Produced by **anaerobic digestion** — bacteria break down organic waste without oxygen
- Can be upgraded to pipeline-quality natural gas
- Sources: landfills, livestock manure, food waste, wastewater treatment

### Other Emerging Biofuels

- **Butanol** — higher energy content than ethanol, can blend at higher ratios
- **Bio-hydrogen** — from algae or gasification of biomass
- **Dimethyl ether (DME)** — clean-burning diesel alternative

## Key Concepts

- **Carbon neutrality (theoretical):** CO₂ released when burning biofuel was recently
  absorbed by the plants that grew the feedstock. Net-zero in theory.
- **In practice:** Production (farming, fertilizer, transport, refining) adds emissions.
- **Drop-in fuels:** Renewable diesel and SAF can directly replace petroleum without
  engine modifications or blending limits.
- **Blend wall:** Most gasoline cars can only take E10 without modification, limiting
  how much ethanol the market can absorb.
