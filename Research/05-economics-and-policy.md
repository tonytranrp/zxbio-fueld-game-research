# Biofuel Economics, Policy & Global Impact

## Market Size & Growth

### Global Biofuel Production (2022)

- Total production: ~45 billion gallons globally
- Ethanol: 64% of production
- Biodiesel: 27%
- Renewable diesel: 8%
- SAF: 0.1%

### Growth Projections

- Global biofuel share of transport fuel: **5.6% (2023) → 6.4% (2030)**
- Will reach ~56.8 billion gallons/year by 2030
- 85% of growth concentrated in: US, Europe, Brazil, Indonesia, India
- Demand increased 27% from 2018 to 2023
- Growth averaged 3.3% per annum over the past decade

### US Production (2022)

| Biofuel | Production (billion gal) | Share |
|---------|------------------------|-------|
| Fuel ethanol | 15.36 | 82% |
| Biodiesel | 1.62 | 9% |
| Renewable diesel | 1.50 | 8% |
| Other biofuels | 0.20 | 1% |
| **Total** | **18.69** | 100% |

- US consumed 17.6 billion gallons
- Net exports: ~1.0 billion gallons (mostly ethanol)
- 187 ethanol refineries, 56 biodiesel plants

## Government Policies Driving Biofuels

### United States

| Policy | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Renewable Fuel Standard (RFS)** | EPA program requiring certain volumes of renewable fuel blended into transportation fuel. Set annual quotas for: conventional biofuel, advanced biofuel, cellulosic biofuel, and biomass-based diesel. |
| **Inflation Reduction Act (IRA)** | Extended biofuel tax credits through 2029. Up to $1.00/gal for non-aviation biofuel, $1.75/gal for sustainable aviation fuel (SAF). |
| **Blender's Tax Credit** | $1.00 per gallon for biodiesel and renewable diesel fuel blenders. |
| **California LCFS** | Low Carbon Fuel Standard — credits for low-carbon fuels, drives massive renewable diesel adoption. |
| **Oregon Clean Fuel Program** | Similar to CA LCFS |
| **Washington Clean Fuel Standard** | Similar to CA LCFS |

### Historical Milestone

**Energy Independence and Security Act of 2007:**
Mandated 36 billion gallons of biofuels annually by 2022 — a sixfold
increase over 2006 levels. Required 79 billion liters of cellulosic
biofuel specifically (which ended up not being met due to technical
challenges).

### Cost Structure of Biofuel Production

**Corn Ethanol Plant Economics (approximate):**

- Corn feedstock: 65-75% of operating cost
- Natural gas (for distillation): 10-15%
- Labor, chemicals, enzymes: 10-15%
- Capital depreciation, maintenance: 5-10%

This means ethanol plant profitability is HIGHLY sensitive to corn prices
and energy costs.

**Biodiesel Plant Economics (approximate):**

- Feedstock (oil/fat): 70-85% of production cost
- Methanol: 5-10%
- Catalyst, chemicals: 3-5%
- Labor, energy, maintenance: 10-15%

Feedstock cost is the single biggest factor.

### E85 vs. Gasoline Price Relationship

E85 is usually cheaper per gallon but delivers fewer miles per gallon.
The "break-even" price for E85 vs. regular gasoline depends on your
vehicle's efficiency loss. Rule of thumb: E85 needs to be ~25% cheaper
to break even with gasoline on a cost-per-mile basis.

## The Food vs. Fuel Debate

### Key Facts

- 36% of US corn goes to ethanol
- One 25-gallon tank of E85 ethanol = corn to feed one person for a year
- Biofuel demand contributed 20-50% to 2006-2008 food price spikes
- Global arable land for biofuels projected to grow from 2.5% to 6% by 2050

### Arguments FOR Biofuels (Counterpoints)

- Only ~1% of corn used for ethanol is the edible sweet corn variety;
  99% is field corn (used for animal feed, industrial products anyway)
- Ethanol plants produce **distillers grains** as a byproduct — a
  high-protein animal feed that replaces some of the corn used
- Second-generation biofuels don't compete with food at all
- Biofuels can use waste streams (used cooking oil, crop residues) that
  would otherwise be thrown away

## The Rebound Effect (Game-Relevant!)

A critical economic concept for a biofuel game:

> When a country produces more biofuel domestically, crop prices rise
> globally. Higher crop prices motivate farmers in other countries to
> convert forests/grasslands to cropland, releasing stored carbon.
> The net result: domestic biofuel policy can INCREASE global
> greenhouse gas emissions through indirect land use change.

This makes the decision-making more complex than "grow more biofuel = good."

## Trade Flows

- US is a net exporter of fuel ethanol (~1.0 billion gal/year)
- US exports corn ethanol to Brazil, imports sugarcane ethanol FROM Brazil
  (sugarcane ethanol has a lower carbon footprint and qualifies for advanced
  biofuel credits)
- EU imports palm oil biodiesel from Indonesia/Malaysia (controversial)
- Asia produces renewable diesel, ships to Europe and California for
  consumption (driven by carbon credit markets)
